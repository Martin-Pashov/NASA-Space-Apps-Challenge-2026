    How this works, data sources and limitations

    Data, fetched live for your field: NASA POWER (25 years of monthly temperature and rain + the last 12 months daily), ESA Copernicus Sentinel-2 via Sentinel Hub (NDVI, SAVI, moisture index NDMI and Bare Soil Index every 10 days for 5 years, clouds removed), ISRIC SoilGrids (texture, organic carbon, pH), NASA SRTM elevation (slope).

    Stress test: every crop is checked against every past season: enough water (season rain + soil water ≥ 75% of crop need) and no heat stress in its critical months. The 2035 outlook shifts past weather along the measured trend. It's a projection, not a forecast.

    Scores (0–100): moisture = how well rain covers crop needs; erosion = months of bare soil between crops; income = typical profit using each year's real outcome; soil = legumes, cover crops and diversity; reliability = share of seasons that passed. Your priorities weight them.

    Limitations: crop values are regional estimates, farming is assumed rain-fed, satellites average a ~500 m box around the point. FieldShift supports your decisions; it doesn't replace your experience or a local agronomist.
