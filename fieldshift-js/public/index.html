    😟 The problem

    NASA and ESA collect enormous amounts of free data about every field on Earth: rain, heat, soil moisture, crop growth. Almost none of it reaches the farmer in a form he can use. Decisions are made from memory, while droughts get more frequent.

    🛰️ What we do

    We read 25 years of NASA climate data, 5 years of Sentinel-2 satellite images of your field, the soil and the slope, plus the weather forecast, and turn them into plain answers: what to sow, what it earns in a normal and a bad year, and what to watch out for.

    ✅ How it helps

    You make an informed decision about your own fields: compare your rotation with 2 better options, get warned about hail, frost, drought and pests in time, and see the evidence behind every number. Free, no account.

      New to crop rotation? The basics in 30 seconds

      • Rotation means growing different crops on the same field in turns, instead of the same crop every year. It stops pests and diseases from building up and keeps the soil healthier.
      • Legumes (peas, chickpeas, soybeans, alfalfa, vetch) take nitrogen from the air and leave it in the soil, so the next crop needs less fertilizer.
      • Cover crops are sown after harvest just to keep the soil covered over winter. They stop erosion, keep moisture and add organic matter.
      • Start simple: a cereal (wheat, barley) followed by an oilseed or legume is easy to manage with standard machinery.

      No signal in the field? How alerts still reach you
      • SMS waits for you. An SMS is stored by the mobile network and delivered the moment your phone catches even a weak 2G signal, which is enough for SMS but not for internet. No app or mobile data needed.
      • Urgent alerts come early. Hail, frost and storm alerts are sent as soon as the forecast shows them (often 1–3 days ahead), usually while you're still in the village, not in the field.
      • The call is repeated if you don't answer, and every alert is also sent by SMS so it's waiting for you.
      • Next step: new phones can receive SMS directly via satellite where there is no mobile network at all (direct-to-phone satellite messaging). FieldShift sends a standard SMS, so it works with that too.

        How this works and where the data comes from

        Data, fetched live for your field: NASA POWER (25 years of monthly temperature and rain + the last 12 months daily), ESA Copernicus Sentinel-2 via Sentinel Hub (greenness NDVI, SAVI, moisture NDMI and bare soil index every 10 days for 5 years, only inside your field outline, clouds removed), ISRIC SoilGrids (texture, organic carbon, pH), NASA SRTM elevation (slope). Field outlines from OpenStreetMap, province borders from Natural Earth.

        Stress test: every crop is checked against every past season: enough rain at sowing (autumn crops), enough water while growing (rain + water stored in the soil), no dry winter and spring (rain from October until the crop's sensitive months at least 80% of what's normal for this field, because a dry winter leaves the soil empty), and no heat stress in its sensitive months. We back-tested this against the Ministry of Agriculture report on the 2020 drought in Dobrich (wheat yields 68% below normal): the model marks 2020 as a bad year. The 2035 outlook shifts past weather along the measured trend. It's a projection, not a forecast.

        Satellite history: crops sown in autumn (wheat, barley, rapeseed) are already green in March–April; spring crops (sunflower, corn) are still bare then. That's how we tell them apart from space.

        Comparing options: profit uses each year's real outcome (bad season = half yield) plus nitrogen left by legumes; a crop after itself loses ~15%. "Better / About the same / Worse fit" combines what you said matters to you.

        Season risk (machine learning): analog-year k-nearest neighbours. For each of the 24 past seasons we measure the same things we can measure today (rain before sowing, rain since October including this month's rain and the 7-day forecast, temperature). The 13 past seasons most similar to this one vote, closer ones count more, mixed with the crop's long-term average so a few years can't give 0% or 100%. Leave-one-out validation predicts every past season from the others; the score is shown next to the result, compared with simply using the long-term average.

        Alerts: the 7-day forecast (ECMWF and DWD ICON models) is checked for hail (thunderstorm with hail codes), heavy rain and floods, frost, heat, drought and storms; the latest Sentinel-2 image is compared with the same weeks in earlier years to spot a sudden drop in greenness (pests, disease, nutrient shortage or drought); and a pest model checks each crop's main pests against the forecast temperature and rain. Levels: URGENT (call + SMS), WARNING (SMS), RECOMMENDATION and INFO (in the app).

        Validation: every data source is checked for missing or impossible values before it's used or saved in the cache, crop names are spell-checked and looked up in the GBIF plant database, and the results are checked for consistency (shown in "Data and result checks").

        Your field's location is only used to fetch public satellite and climate data. No account, no names; recent fields are saved only in your browser.
